# EACL 2027 response draft

We thank both reviewers for recognizing the practical value of the released family and for identifying weaknesses in our claims and reporting. We have revised the contribution around observed quality–serving trade-offs and audited the original serving and evaluation artifacts.

**Novelty and quality claims.** We agree that the claim to the first general-purpose MoE embedding encoder was incorrect. We removed it and added Nomic Embed v2 (Nussbaum and Duderstadt, 2025, arXiv:2502.07972) and MoEE (Li and Zhou, ICLR 2025), distinguishing MoEE's training-free construction from a trained bidirectional encoder. We also removed “without sacrificing embedding quality.” Our MoE has the highest reported scores within this family, but Qwen3-Embedding-4B scores higher on English, multilingual and code. Differences in backbone, capacity, embedding dimension and runtime profile prevent isolating sparsity or establishing an equal-quality efficiency advantage. The contribution is the released family and its empirical characterization, rather than a new MoE or distillation method.

**Serving protocol and compact model.** We located the original script and per-repeat timing files. Each run used one H100 80GB HBM3, BF16, vLLM 0.24.0, PyTorch 2.11.0+cu129, CUDA runtime 12.9, Transformers 5.13.0 and Python 3.12.13. The offline synchronous embedding benchmark uses three warm-ups and seven timed repetitions per setting. Throughput is batch size × input length divided by median call time. Inputs are synthetic random token IDs of exact length, without padding; loading, tokenization and input generation are excluded. Appendix C specifies input generation, timing boundaries, pooling, engine settings and the special MoE execution profile. These are encoder-call measurements, not an online deployment study. The external baseline is `nvidia/llama-embed-nemotron-8b`.

The compact model was present under a legacy “600M” label; the current checkpoint at that path has 483,717,632 parameters and its weight-file hash matches the public 480M release. Its batch-16 throughput at 512/1024/2048 tokens is 356.2k/425.7k/437.1k tokens/s. We added it and corrected all claims that MoE is the fastest family member. At 1024 tokens, batch-1 median latencies are 7.29/13.24/30.16 ms for 480M/3B/MoE; whole-batch-16 medians are 38.49/179.05/143.13 ms. Thus, MoE exceeds 3B batch throughput, while 3B has lower single-input latency. All three lengths are reported, including the smaller 13% advantage at 2048, without attributing the decline to an unmeasured bottleneck. Historical logs lack contemporaneous checkpoint hashes and complete engine startup output; these limits are explicit.

**Latency and memory.** Appendix C reports descriptive p50/p95 values from seven preserved timings, which do not support robust tail-latency or production-SLO claims. Appendix D separates measured PyTorch peak allocated memory from weight storage and device-wide memory. Dense vLLM client counters miss worker allocations and are marked unavailable, not zero. Transformers memory is identified as a separate backend. Complete vLLM device peaks and adequately repeated tail measurements remain limitations; we do not claim new runs have been completed. Full BF16 weight-storage estimates are 0.97/6.30/20.95 decimal GB for 480M/3B/MoE: sparse activation does not imply a smaller full checkpoint.

**Training data and statistics.** We correct the original statement: embedding-training sources are open-source Nemotron and KaLM data; the proprietary component is GigaChat backbone pre-training. Appendix B names HIT-TMG/KaLM-embedding-pretrain-data, KaLM-Embedding/KaLM-embedding-finetuning-data and nvidia/embed-nemotron-dataset-v1 and distinguishes their published roles. The authors confirm no overlap between embedding-training and evaluation examples; a detailed checking procedure was not located in the inspected records, and no new audit is claimed here. We now report actual logged prepared-dataset sizes and checkpoint consumed-sample counters for archived compact-model branches, rather than file counts alone. For example, KaLM pre-training records 299,034,744 prepared rows and 299,024,384 consumed samples; a completed multitask run records 2,439,873 prepared rows and 7,317,504 consumed samples over three epochs. The table also covers Giga/F2LLM pre-training and four fine-tuning branches. These are branch-specific records, not unique-example totals or a reconstructed final-release mixture. In particular, the multitask record is from August 3, not the missing August 4/7 release-related runs. Language proportions and complete final merge provenance remain unavailable. We also disclose the archived F2LLM pre-training global batch of 1024, distinct from the core recipe's 16,384.

**Backbones, teacher and distillation.** The dense models use the Qwen3 architecture family but were initialized from proprietary GigaChat checkpoints, rather than public Qwen3 checkpoints of those sizes. Appendix A gives dimensions, expert configuration and an active-weight convention yielding approximately 1.63B; “10B-A1.8B” remains the release identifier. Release router fields do not establish the historical balancing loss or token-dropping policy, which remain unverified. The teacher is a 10B MoE encoder: its query/candidate vectors were precomputed, and the 480M student matches their eight-candidate similarity distributions with KL weight 0.3 and temperature 0.05. Complete teacher-cache and merge lineage remains incomplete.

We agree that +0.09/+0.12/+0.22 single-run differences do not establish reliable KD gains. The audit additionally found different merged initialization paths and microbatch sizes, despite the same global batch size. The current initialization files differ in 308 of 310 tensors; contemporaneous hashes linking them to the historical training runs are absent. We relabelled Table 7 as a descriptive comparison and removed causal and “consistent gains” wording. No no-KD multilingual result or independent training-seed series was recovered. A unified evaluation has only passed CPU preflight and is not reported as completed. A separate KaLM-12B-teacher fine-tuning pair was found, but it does not establish a controlled 10B-teacher ablation for the final releases.

**Multilingual aggregation correction.** We withdraw the original 480M value 56.97. The recovered notebook can carry a previous task's score into LEMBPasskeyRetrieval because its length-specific split names are not recognized. Replaying the archived order gives 56.970884; correctly aggregating all 131 tasks and 46,933 selected subset/split cells gives 56.799822. The working revision reports 56.80 with an explicit archived-cache marker and Appendix F documents the defect and scope. This is CPU reanalysis of a mixed-version cache without an immutable checkpoint binding, not a new inference result or a complete public leaderboard score. The numerical match is not proof of the paper-time invocation. The corrected cache-based gap to 3B is approximately 7.09 points. The 3B/MoE selected-cell means match the public 63.89/65.64; the notebook defect does not invalidate those independently sourced aggregates.

**Russian score provenance, FRIDA and publication details.** Original 480M Russian 70.98 and public 70.96 are reproduced from identical results on all 23 tasks: the original notebook prefers test splits, while public aggregation includes validation and test for the two MASSIVE tasks (70.978526 versus 70.959130; Appendix E). We use dated public `meanTask` aggregates for release comparisons and preserve the original common protocol for the two training variants. The compact model's practical contribution is comparable reported Russian quality with substantially fewer parameters and additional multilingual coverage: 70.96 versus FRIDA's 70.95 at about 42% fewer parameters using reported sizes of 480M versus 823M. We have made this a separate contribution in the introduction and restored the emphasis in the abstract and conclusion. This contextual size–quality comparison does not interpret the tiny score difference as a performance gain or assert statistical equivalence. The same checkpoint is evaluated on English, multilingual and code tasks, with the archived multilingual score's provenance explicitly stated; we make no inference about FRIDA's unreported multilingual capabilities. Release model cards specify MIT. The previous publication is available at ACL Anthology, DOI [10.18653/v1/2025.bsnlp-1.3](https://aclanthology.org/2025.bsnlp-1.3/); authors and metadata have been corrected. We also corrected Wen-tau Yih, model names and the unexplained release suffix.
